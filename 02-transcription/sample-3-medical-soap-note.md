## CLINICAL ENCOUNTER NOTE

### SUBJECTIVE
**Chief Complaint:** Acute substernal chest pressure radiating to the left shoulder and jaw for approximately 2.5 hours.

**History of Present Illness (HPI):**  
The patient is a 56-year-old male with a documented medical history of essential hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and type 2 diabetes mellitus. He presents to the Emergency Department reporting severe, crushing chest pain described as 8/10 in intensity, beginning abruptly while at rest at approximately 06:30 hours. Associated symptoms include diaphoresis, mild dyspnea, and nausea. No prior syncope or palpitations. The patient administered one sublingual nitroglycerin tablet en route via emergency medical services with negligible symptomatic relief.

**Past Medical History (PMH):**
1. Essential Hypertension (10 years) — managed with Lisinopril 20 mg PO daily.
2. Hyperlipidemia (8 years) — managed with Atorvastatin 40 mg PO daily.
3. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (6 years) — managed with Metformin 1000 mg PO BID.

**Allergies:** No Known Drug Allergies (NKDA).  
**Social History:** Non-smoker, socially consumes 1–2 glasses of wine per week. Sedentary occupation.

---

### OBJECTIVE
**Vital Signs:**  
- **Blood Pressure:** 162/96 mm Hg (Right Arm, seated)  
- **Heart Rate:** 104 beats per minute (sinus tachycardia)  
- **Respiratory Rate:** 22 breaths per minute  
- **Oxygen Saturation:** 94% on room air  
- **Temperature:** 36.8 °C (98.2 °F)

**Physical Examination:**  
- **General:** Alert, oriented x3, visibly anxious and diaphoretic.  
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardic, regular rhythm. Normal S1 and S2 heart sounds. No distinct murmurs, friction rubs, or gallops identified. Peripheral pulses present and symmetric bilaterally. No peripheral pedal edema.  
- **Pulmonary:** Bilateral breath sounds clear to auscultation without audible wheezing, rales, or rhonchi. Symmetric chest wall expansion.

**Diagnostic Evaluations (Initial):**  
- **12-Lead Electrocardiogram (ECG):** Demonstrated marked ST-segment elevation (>2 mm) in leads V2 through V4, accompanied by reciprocal ST-segment depressions in inferior leads II, III, and aVF. Findings are consistent with an acute anteroseptal myocardial infarction (STEMI).  
- **Serum Cardiac Biomarkers:** Cardiac Troponin I markedly elevated at 14.8 ng/mL (Reference: < 0.04 ng/mL).

---

### ASSESSMENT
1. **Acute ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI), Anteroseptal Distribution:** Strongly suggestive of acute occlusion of the proximal Left Anterior Descending (LAD) coronary artery.
2. **Hypertensive Urgency secondary to acute ischemic stress.**
3. **Controlled Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.**

---

### PLAN
1. **Immediate Intervention:** Immediate activation of the Cardiac Catheterization Laboratory for emergent coronary angiography and primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with goal door-to-balloon time under 90 minutes.
2. **Antiplatelet Therapy:** Administered Aspirin 325 mg chewed stat; loaded with Ticagrelor 180 mg PO stat.
3. **Anticoagulation:** Intravenous unfractionated heparin bolus administered per body weight protocol (60 units/kg).
4. **Hemodynamic Management:** Intravenous Nitroglycerin infusion initiated at 10 mcg/min, titrated to blood pressure and symptom resolution.
5. **Disposition:** Post-procedure transfer to the Cardiac Intensive Care Unit (CICU) for continuous telemetry and post-PCI monitoring.
